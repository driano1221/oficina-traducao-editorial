Read the pattern,
question the claim

A figure can reveal the shape of an error. It cannot, by itself, establish a causal explanation.

What the figure does and does not say

Samples B and C lie on opposite sides of the line. Their signed errors cancel in the average, although neither prediction is exact. The plotted distances make that distinction visible.

Nothing here shows that changing x would cause a change in y. A causal claim requires assumptions about how the data were generated, not just a well-fitting line.2

A short reading checklist

  • Check the scale and the meaning of each symbol.
  • Read the caption before interpreting a trend.
  • Separate a descriptive pattern from a causal claim.

2. The line is a fixed reference rule, not a model fitted to these four points. The graphic and text were created specifically for this example. ↩
